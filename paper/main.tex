\begin{abstract}
Streaming ASR commits to an early transcription before later dialogue can disambiguate acoustically confusable words. We present ReTrace, an automatic agentic controller that uses later conversational semantics to selectively revisit, rather than globally re-decode, an earlier audio region. ReTrace maintains immutable first-pass observations and closed candidate sets from ASR uncertainty. A semantic trigger invokes candidate-conditioned audio verification; a revision is committed only when temporal semantic evidence and local audio evidence support the same candidate by a calibrated margin. The resulting transcript is reconstructed from append-only revision events. We provide a reproducible evaluation protocol that measures transcription error together with ambiguity resolution, over-correction, verification cost, and revision latency.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}streaming ASR, audio-language models, contextual disambiguation, selective verification\end{IEEEkeywords}
\section{Introduction}
Large language models improve ASR rescoring and contextual correction, but text-only correction can prefer a plausible phrase that was not spoken. Audio-conditioned correction and constrained decoding reduce this risk \cite{seal,strescoring}. ReTrace addresses a distinct streaming setting: a later turn reveals that an earlier uncertain span has multiple semantic interpretations. Instead of rewriting the history from text, it selectively re-verifies the corresponding historical audio.
\section{Method}
For chunk $t$, a first-pass recognizer produces $o_t=(h_t,\mathcal{N}_t,q_t,\tau_t)$: a one-best transcript, an N-best set, uncertainty scores, and time boundaries. Low-confidence or disagreeing spans form a closed set $\mathcal{C}_i$. When a later raw turn provides a verbatim semantic cue, the controller chooses \texttt{WAIT}, \texttt{RELISTEN}, or \texttt{REVISE}. \texttt{RELISTEN} scores only $\mathcal{C}_i$ on the cropped historical audio. Let $s_i(c)$, $a_i(c)$, and $p_i(c)$ denote semantic support, audio-verification support, and first-pass prior. ReTrace uses
\begin{equation}
F_i(c)=\alpha a_i(c)+\beta s_i(c)+\gamma p_i(c).
\end{equation}
It appends a revision only if the best candidate has a valid later quote, $F_i(c^*)\geq\theta$, and a margin over all alternatives of at least $\delta$. Otherwise it retains the original ASR observation. The displayed transcript is $y_t=\operatorname{Replay}(o_{1:t},E_{1:t})$, where $E$ is an append-only event sequence.
\section{Experimental Protocol}
We evaluate conversational audio containing early acoustically confusable mentions and later resolving context. Baselines are first-pass ASR, text-only N-best correction, audio-language-model rescoring, and full historical re-decoding. We report CER/WER, ambiguity-resolution accuracy, revision precision, over-correction rate, mean revision latency, and average re-listened audio seconds. Ablations remove semantic triggering, audio verification, and selective scheduling.

\section{Limitations}
ReTrace depends on accurate time boundaries and a capable audio verifier. It abstains when audio and semantic evidence disagree; this safety behavior can increase revision latency.
\section{Conclusion}
ReTrace turns later conversational context into a selective request for historical audio verification. Its closed candidate set and dual-evidence gate target semantic ambiguity while limiting both unsupported edits and repeated audio computation.
